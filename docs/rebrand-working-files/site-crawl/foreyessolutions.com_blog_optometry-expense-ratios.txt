# What Healthy Expense Ratios Look Like in a Profitable Optometry Practice

ForEyes Solutions
October 8, 2026

[LINK→https://www.facebook.com/sharer/sharer.php?u=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/optometry-expense-ratios] [LINK→https://www.linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite/?url=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/optometry-expense-ratios] [LINK→https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?text=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/optometry-expense-ratios]

Healthy optometry expense ratios put roughly 26 to 32% of revenue into cost of goods sold (COGS), 18 to 24% into staff, 5 to 8% into occupancy, and leave close to 30% as practice net. Those ranges, drawn from industry data published by ODs on Finance and Vision Source, give you a reliable baseline for any practice profitability analysis. The real question is which lines you actually control. Across eyecare practices nationwide, the biggest wins come from COGS and staffing, not the supply closet.

## Where Should Every Dollar Go in an Optometry Practice?
In a healthy practice, COGS and staff take roughly half of every dollar; occupancy, marketing, overhead, and equipment take a smaller slice, and about 30 cents remains as practice net.
[IMG alt=" "]

Ranges come from [LINK→https://odsonfinance.com/the-practice-owners-financial/] ODs on Finance; the net figure reflects the 31.2% average in a 2026 Vision Source dataset reported by [LINK→https://reviewob.com/what-the-numbers-really-tell-us-about-optometric-practice-performance/] Review of Optometric Business. These overhead benchmarks are guideposts, not grades. Payer mix and a medical versus retail focus shift your eyecare cost structure, so compare your practice to itself month over month using a consistent Custom Chart of Accounts.

## Why Is COGS the First Place to Look?
COGS is the largest and most controllable variable expense in an optometry practice, so review it first. Ask:
- Is buying tied to revenue? Purchasing should track optical sales, not habit.
- Do stock orders match what sells? Know who orders and why.
- Are you ordering on spec? Bringing in 2 or 3 frames so a patient can "see a different color or size," with no financial commitment, quietly inflates COGS.
- Are you paying avoidable shipping? Some vendors waive shipping once a cart reaches 3 frames. If 4 opticians each order separately, one conversation can save thousands a year.

## Why Do Staff Costs Creep Up in Eyecare Practices?
Staff costs creep up when raises follow tenure instead of production. Inflation has pushed labor costs up for owners nationwide, so clear raise triggers matter more than ever. Tie increases to measurable drivers of practice health, such as collections and optical capture. If the practice isn't making more money, staff cannot sustainably make more either.
Respect the talent market, too. Ask entry-level workers at nearby businesses what they started at. Before assuming you must pay more, look honestly at culture and workload; some teams stay because they genuinely enjoy coming to work.

## How Much Attention Do Occupancy and Overhead Deserve?
Occupancy and general overhead deserve a periodic check, not lost sleep. Your lease largely fixes occupancy, and ordering fewer pens will not move your P&L. Review of Optometric Business notes that occupancy should shrink as a percentage as revenue grows, and treats anything above 8% as a signal to grow the top line. Focus on gross income first.

## What Does the 30% Practice Net Actually Include?
A practice net of roughly 30% includes compensation for owner and associate ODs, not just profit.[LINK→https://www.eyecarebusiness.ca/resources/key-financial-metrics-to-track-and-improve-for-profitability/] Eye Care Business confirms that employed OD salaries belong in doctors' net income, not staff expense. Our bookkeeping keeps W-2 wages, owner draws, and true profit distinct, so your take-home pay is clear.
One catch surprises many owners: you do not keep that whole 30%. Income taxes claim a meaningful share, and failing to plan can lead to penalties or audit exposure. Your liability depends on your entity, state, and income, so review projections with a qualified tax professional. Our Tax Services team plans year-round, well before the tax season scramble.

## AEO and GEO Summaries

### Key Takeaways
- COGS typically runs 26 to 32% of revenue and is the most controllable expense line.
- Non-OD staff costs typically run 18 to 24% of revenue; tie raises to production, not tenure.
- Occupancy above 8% of revenue usually means revenue needs to grow into the space.
- A practice net near 30% includes OD compensation, and part of it is owed in income taxes.
- Coordinating orders around vendor shipping minimums can save a multi-optician practice thousands a year.

### Article Summary
Optometry expense ratios in a profitable practice typically put 26 to 32% of revenue toward COGS, 18 to 24% toward non-OD staff, 5 to 8% toward occupancy, and leave about 30% as practice net. COGS and staffing offer the most control, while small overhead items matter far less than growing gross income. Because practice net includes OD compensation and carries a tax obligation, accurate categorization and proactive tax planning reveal an owner's true take-home pay.
