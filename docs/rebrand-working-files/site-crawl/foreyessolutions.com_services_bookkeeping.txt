## Books That Show What Your Practice Actually Earns

Exams, optical sales, and contact lenses all deposit into the same bank account, which makes it hard to see which part of your practice carries its weight. Our bookkeeping for optometrists in Nashville, TN, and nationwide sorts every dollar into a structure built for eyecare, so your monthly P&L reads like a story you can act on. It comes from Wade Weisz, OD, and his 25-plus years in the owner's seat.

## Full-Service Eyecare Bookkeeping

Good books explain your practice instead of merely recording it. We manage your daily transactions, code them to a structure designed for eyecare, keep your general ledger current, and deliver reviewed reports on a fixed schedule. The same team also supports your [LINK→https://foreyessolutions.com/services/accounting] accounting and [LINK→https://foreyessolutions.com/services/payroll] payroll needs, so nothing is lost between bookkeeping and the work that follows.

[IMG alt="icon"]

### Custom Chart of Accounts

Most bookkeeping software lumps income into a few broad lines and costs into a handful of buckets. Ours separates medical exam revenue from optical sales, and optical cost of goods from overhead, so you can see which services earn their keep. It also calculates Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) automatically, the valuation metric buyers and lenders ask about first.

[IMG alt="icon"]

### Accurate Transaction Categorization

Every deposit, vendor payment, and card swipe is coded to the right account the first time, whether it arrives through a bank feed or a manual import. Each month we match every account to its statement, so the balance in your books is the balance in your bank. The IRS expects books that clearly show gross income, deductions, and credits, and [LINK→https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep] its recordkeeping guidance describes the supporting documents that back them up.

[IMG alt="icon"]

### A Clean General Ledger, Every Month

Your general ledger is the foundation every report sits on, and small slips compound quickly. Each month we review adjustments, accruals, and uncategorized items so the ledger closes clean and stays consistent from one period to the next. Equipment purchases are recorded as assets with the details needed to calculate depreciation under [LINK→https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946] IRS Publication 946, which keeps your tax work accurate later.

[IMG alt="icon"]

### Reviewed Reports by the 10th Business Day

Speed matters only when the numbers are right. Before any P&L or balance sheet reaches your client portal, a dedicated quality control manager reviews it. By the 10th business day each month, you'll have complete, reviewed, and accurate financials for the month prior, far faster than many other Nashville, TN bookkeepers can deliver. Most firms don't close the books until the end of the month.
