# How to Improve Cash Flow in Your Eyecare Practice Without Taking on More Patients

ForEyes Solutions
June 16, 2026

[LINK→https://www.facebook.com/sharer/sharer.php?u=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/how-to-improve-cash-flow-eyecare-practice] [LINK→https://www.linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite/?url=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/how-to-improve-cash-flow-eyecare-practice] [LINK→https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?text=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/how-to-improve-cash-flow-eyecare-practice] 

[IMG alt=""]

[IMG alt=""]

More patients isn't the only path to better cash flow. For most eyecare professionals, the real opportunity is hiding in plain sight: slower billing cycles, outdated fee schedules, excess inventory, and uncollected balances are quietly draining practice liquidity every single week. Here are six practical strategies to improve optometry cash flow management without opening a single additional appointment slot.

## Speed Up Your Revenue Cycle
The fastest way to recover lost revenue is to get claims out faster and chase denials harder. That starts with organizational clarity: separate your internal workflows from your external billing processes, and protect the people handling this work from the constant pull of patient care demands.
Just as important as the structure is the person in the role. Your billing specialist needs to be computer-proficient, compliance-minded, and genuinely motivated to recover every dollar owed, including that missing $10 copay. If that internal drive isn't there, revenue cycle improvement stalls regardless of what systems you put in place.

## Update Your Fee Schedule Regularly
An outdated fee schedule is a silent leak in your revenue. If you're not sure where yours stands, start with your Medicare Fee Schedule as a baseline. (If we're not already managing your billing, reach out and we can pull that for you.) Review your full schedule at least annually and adjust where payers have shifted. Even small adjustments across hundreds of transactions add up to meaningful gains in eyecare profitability.
Liquidate Excess Inventory, Don't Just Reduce It
Here's the important distinction: you've already paid for that aging frame inventory. The goal isn't to slowly reduce it; it's to convert it into cash now. Mark it down. Run a staff spiff. Do whatever it takes to move it, and then don't replace those frames.
The deeper fix is evaluating your frame lines regularly and buying based on actual sales data, not on vendor relationships or personal preference at the buying table. Practice liquidity depends on inventory that moves, not inventory that looks good on the board.

## Collect Copays and Balances at Checkout, Every Time
This is the baseline expectation, not a best practice. The practices that do it best go further: they prepare patients for what they'll owe before they ever reach the checkout desk. When a patient knows their balance in advance, the conversation at checkout is smooth for both staff and patient. A great clinical experience shouldn't end on a tense financial note because the fee was a surprise.

## Review Vendor Contracts and Invoices Regularly
True renegotiation opportunities have narrowed as prices have risen across the board, but that doesn't mean your vendor costs are locked in. Start by pulling your last three months of invoices across labs, suppliers, and software subscriptions. Look specifically for shipping surcharges, price-per-unit increases that weren't communicated, and tools with overlapping functionality. Even when you can't negotiate a lower rate, catching a billing error or eliminating a redundant subscription protects your margins in ways that compound over time.

## Use a Rolling Cash Flow Forecast Built on One Metric
Forecasting practice cash flow doesn't have to be complicated. The most actionable approach is to track your average revenue per doctor per full clinic day, then use that number to project income for each upcoming quarter based on how many days each doctor is scheduled to work.
This single metric cuts through the noise. It grounds your cash flow optimization in real operational data, gives you a quarterly planning tool that's straightforward to update, and helps you spot gaps far enough in advance to act.
Cash flow optimization in an eyecare practice is largely a discipline problem, not a volume problem. Tighten your revenue cycle, price accurately, turn inventory into cash, collect what you're owed, watch your vendor costs, and forecast with a metric you trust. That's a stronger financial foundation than any amount of additional scheduling can build.
