# Why Your Accounts Receivable Is Growing and How to Bring It Under Control

ForEyes Solutions
July 2, 2026

[LINK→https://www.facebook.com/sharer/sharer.php?u=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/why-accounts-receivable-is-growing] [LINK→https://www.linkedin.com/sharing/share-offsite/?url=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/why-accounts-receivable-is-growing] [LINK→https://twitter.com/intent/tweet?text=https://foreyessolutions.com//blog/why-accounts-receivable-is-growing] 

[IMG alt=""]

[IMG alt=""]

If your accounts receivable report keeps growing, you're not alone — and you're not just looking at a number on a spreadsheet. That number represents services your practice has already delivered and hasn't been paid for. The longer it sits, the harder it is to collect. Understanding why A/R creeps up, and what to do about it, is one of the highest-leverage skills in eyecare practice management.

## Why A/R Creeps Up in the First Place
Accounts receivable don’t balloon overnight. It builds slowly through a handful of recurring problems: claims submitted late, eligibility errors missed at the front desk, patient balances left uncollected after the visit, and denials that get filed away instead of being worked.
Every one of these has a root cause inside your practice. That's actually good news — because what happens internally can be changed internally. Unpaid claims in eyecare are almost always traceable to a process gap, not bad luck.

## Know Your Aging Buckets and What Healthy Looks Like
Your A/R aging report tells you exactly how long money has been sitting uncollected. A healthy distribution for most optometry practices looks like this:
[IMG alt=" "]

If your 90+ bucket is growing, that's where revenue leakage becomes real. Insurance reimbursement delays compound quickly past the 90-day mark, and patient balances that age out rarely come back in full. Review your optometry A/R aging report at least monthly. Know your numbers.

## Verify Insurance Eligibility Before the Patient Is Seen
This one is non-negotiable: do not work for free. Verifying insurance eligibility before every appointment is the single most effective way to prevent unpaid claims.
When eligibility is confirmed in advance, your team knows exactly what's covered, what requires a copay or patient portion, and whether prior authorization is needed. Catching a coverage issue before the patient is seated costs nothing. Discovering it after the claim is denied costs you time, money, and a reset of the collections clock.
Prevention beats collection every time.

## Catch and Prevent Common Denial Triggers
Every denial that comes through your practice is a lesson — if you let it be one. When a claim is denied, the right question isn't just "how do we fix this?" It's "how do we make sure this never happens again?"
Use denials to audit your intake process. Was it a data entry error? A missing modifier? An eligibility oversight? Trace it back to the source, correct the internal workflow, and train accordingly. The practices with the lowest denial rates treat each one as an operational memo from the payer.

## Work Denials Weekly — Better Yet, Avoid Them Altogether
Working denials monthly is better than not working them at all. But monthly is still too slow. Most commercial payers are required to pay a clean claim within 60 days. Once a claim is denied — even if your resubmission is perfect — the clock resets to 180 days. The payer is under no urgency to prioritize it.
That math should change how your team thinks about clean claims. Basic, accurate data entry from your front desk is the difference between thousands of dollars collected on time and thousands sitting in aging buckets collecting dust. Work denials weekly. And invest heavily in avoiding them in the first place.

## Keep Your A/R Under Control Before It Keeps You
Strong accounts receivable management doesn't require an overhaul of the billing department. It requires consistent habits: verify eligibility before the visit, submit clean claims the first time, work denials on a weekly cadence, and review your aging report with real attention.
Your A/R report reflects the health of your internal operations as much as it reflects payer behavior. The practices that control their receivables treat every touchpoint — from front-desk check-in to claim submission — as a revenue event worth getting right.
