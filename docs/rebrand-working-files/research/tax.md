## What a client-facing "we no longer offer tax; here is how we work with your CPA" message needs to cover

Derived from what these pages promised. Items marked (decision) are not answered anywhere on the current site and need the owner's input before copy is written.

1. The plain statement. ForEyes no longer prepares, files, plans or advises on taxes, for the practice or for the owner personally. Say it once, early, without hedging.
2. Exactly what stops, named in the old site's own terms, so no client assumes a piece survives:
   - business tax return preparation and filing ("we file everything on your behalf")
   - the owner's individual return ("both your business and your personal returns", sold "as part of your larger business package")
   - annual tax review
   - tax planning and "strategizing": salaries and distributions, entity structure, retirement contributions, depreciation / Section 179 timing
   - tax forecasting and estimated payment planning
   - tax advisory on purchases, expansion, partner transitions, ownership changes
   - monitoring of tax regulation changes
3. Timing. Effective date; the last tax year ForEyes will file; what happens to returns in progress, extensions, and any quarterly estimates already scheduled (decision).
4. Existing clients on a bundled "business package". How the package and its price change once tax is removed; whether anything is refunded or re-quoted (decision). The pricing calculator must match.
5. Records handoff. How clients get copies of prior returns, workpapers, depreciation schedules and carryforward information, and how long ForEyes keeps them (decision).
6. Who does the taxes now. Either "your own CPA" or a referral list; say which, and whether ForEyes endorses or is paid by anyone it refers to (decision).
7. Why the change. A reason that is true and benefits the client (focus on books and insurance billing for eyecare) rather than an apology. It must not contradict itself: the old site argued that separating bookkeeping from tax causes "missed deductions, reporting inconsistencies, and avoidable tax liabilities", so the message has to explain why a clean handoff avoids that.
8. What ForEyes still does that makes the CPA's job easy (this is the "tax-ready books" promise; confirm each before publishing):
   - books reconciled and closed monthly, with a year-end close by a stated date (decision on the date)
   - a year-end package for the CPA (financial statements, general ledger, fixed-asset purchases, loan balances, owner draws and compensation) (decision on contents)
   - accountant access to the accounting file, and a named ForEyes contact who answers the CPA's questions directly so the owner is not the go-between
   - mid-year numbers on request so the CPA can do planning and estimates
   - payroll run through Gusto and bills through Bill.com (both named on the current site); state clearly whether payroll tax filings, W-2s and 1099s are handled inside the payroll and AP services, since clients will read "no tax" as including those (decision)
   - whether sales tax on optical sales is in or out of scope (decision)
9. Where the line is. ForEyes records and reports; the CPA advises and files. ForEyes staff will not answer "can I deduct this?" or "should I be an S corp?", and will route those to the CPA. Tax notices (IRS or state) go to the CPA; ForEyes supplies the supporting records.
10. What the client must do. Introduce their CPA (name, email), or ask for a referral; sign any access or information-sharing consent; know the dates for the year-end package.
11. Reassurance on continuity. Same team, same founder (Wade Weisz, OD), same phone 844-505-3937 and hello@foreyessolutions.com, same client portal; bookkeeping and billing service is unchanged.
12. Where the message lives. A short section on the Bookkeeping page ("Working with your CPA"), an FAQ entry ("Do you do taxes?", "Will you work with my current CPA?", "Can you recommend a CPA?", "Do you still handle payroll tax filings and 1099s?"), an email to current tax clients, and the landing target for the redirected tax URLs.
13. Wording guardrails. Avoid "maximize deductions", "reduce your tax liability", "tax strategy", "tax experts", "advisors" and "under one roof" anywhere on the new site; these imply the dropped service. "Tax-ready books" and "ready for your CPA" are fine because they describe bookkeeping.
